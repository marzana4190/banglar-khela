Ha-du-du (হা-ডু-ডু)

Category: Outdoor (Land-Based) Region: Widespread (Dhaka, Faridpur, Mymensingh) Status: Endangered Folk Variant

Historical & Cultural Background

Ha-du-du is one of Bangladesh's most iconic rural folk games, recognized as the ancestral predecessor to modern Kabaddi. Traditionally played after harvest season on soft mud or open village grounds, it served as a social gathering event reinforcing community solidarity.

Traditional Rhymes & Folk Chants (ছড়া)

A core element of living knowledge in Ha-du-du is the continuous chant held in a single breath by the raider:

"হাডুডু ডূ ডূ, সাপের বিষে মইরা যামু...
হাত দিয়া ধরলে পা দিয়া মারমু!"
— Recorded in Faridpur District (1978 Oral Archive)

Rules & Mechanics

Two teams of equal players occupy opposite courts divided by a center line (*midline*). A raider from one team crosses into enemy territory chanting "Ha-du-du" continuously without taking a breath, attempting to tag opponents and return safely across the midline.
